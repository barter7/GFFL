*"Even the very wise cannot see all ends." — Gandalf, in The Fellowship of the Ring, to anyone already measuring Harry's finger*

Welcome back one and all to the ninth weekly installment of the GFFL Recap! The Fellowship of the Ringless is 9-0, with 1,274.04 points and an 86-13 record against the whole league week by week, and this week it posted its lowest score of the season and won by 28. So the commissioner has put a question to the commissioner: are we handing the ring to Harry, or what? I looked it up. Harry won't like the answer. Elsewhere, the last-place team benched a five-touchdown quarterback and lost the week's only flippable game, and I benched the winning touchdown of an overtime game. Again.

## The Fellowship of the Ringless 111.1 — Whistlin' Willy's 82.86

The Fellowship's worst week of the year: five teams outscored it, and none of them were playing it. Matthew Golden caught all 10 of his targets for 164 yards and a touchdown in Green Bay's 45–27 win in New England (27.4). Ka'imi Fairbairn kicked three field goals and five extra points in Houston's 44–22 rout of the Chargers (17), Audric Estime scored the touchdown that put New Orleans ahead of Cleveland with a minute left in regulation (14.2), and Jahmyr Gibbs scored the only touchdown of Detroit's 19–9 win in Miami (13.4). Jared Goff threw no touchdowns (5.3). On the bench, Travis Kelce scored 15.3 and Josh Allen outscored Goff by 7.56: a league-high 24.76 points left there. It won by 28.24.

So, the ring. Nine straight wins is the second-best start in league records, which go back to 2017. The best was Mike's 12-0 in 2024. Mike finished that season 13-2 and second; Kerley won the title. Harry has his own history: in nine seasons of records he has finished second three times (2020, 2021 and 2023) and first zero times, and last year he went 3-12. There's a reason the team is called the Ringless. And the Fellowship has played nine of its eleven possible opponents. The two it hasn't: The Irreversible Binomials, owned by Mike, now 8-1, and Smush Happens, owned by Kerley, who won in 2024 and finished second last year. So no, nobody gets a ring in week 9. But I'd keep a box handy.

Whistlin' Willy's best possible lineup was 98.38, so it was never getting there. Javonte Williams ran for 101 yards and the go-ahead touchdown with 1:10 left in Dallas's 27–23 win in Indianapolis (20.4), and Jake Bates kicked four field goals in Miami (15). Caleb Williams threw two touchdowns and four interceptions in Chicago's 27–24 win over Tampa Bay (12.76), and both touchdowns went to other rosters in this league (Rome Odunze and Luther Burden III). Patrick Mahomes, benched for the third straight week, threw two touchdowns against the Jets (16.48). Both went to my Kenneth Walker III. Thank you, Willy's. Tyler Allgeier, last week's 33.6 on the bench, got the flex start this time and ran 17 times for 46 yards (5.6); Blake Corum scored on the bench (11.8). 5-4.

## Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him 101.48 — Injury Reservoir 85.74

The closest game of the week (15.74), and the only one a better lineup would have flipped, decided at quarterback by the team that lost.

Injury Reservoir has two quarterbacks and keeps picking the wrong one. In week 7 it started C.J. Stroud, and Kirk Cousins outscored him on the bench. In week 8 Stroud was on a bye and Cousins scored 28.18. This week it stayed with Cousins, who threw one touchdown and three interceptions in a 38–17 loss in San Francisco (12.64). The touchdown went to Brock Bowers, who plays for the Binomials. Stroud threw for **384 yards and five touchdowns** against the Chargers from the bench (35.36), the second-best score of the week by anyone, starting or not. Start Stroud and Injury Reservoir wins by 6.98. Instead it's 1-8 and alone in last again. Cairo Santos won Chicago's game with a field goal at 0:03 (9), and the Bears defense scored a return touchdown (13). Breece Hall (10.2) and DJ Moore (5.1), both on Tee'ing Up A 6-7 Record last week, made their debuts. And Adonai Mitchell, last week's 142-yard revenge story, is now on this bench. He scored zero.

Thats My Commish won with a quarterback who was benched halfway through his own game. J.J. McCarthy threw for 127 yards, gave way to Winston for the third quarter and came back for the fourth of the Giants' 19–11 win in Philadelphia (7.28). Jonah Coleman scored the touchdown that put Denver ahead of Carolina for good early in the fourth quarter (16.8), and Spencer Shrader kicked a 50-yarder to put the Colts ahead of Dallas with 1:41 left (16). It lasted 31 seconds. The Chiefs defense held the Jets to four field goals (12). Harold Fannin Jr. caught a 39-yard touchdown with 1:11 left in overtime to pull Cleveland within one, and Cleveland's two-point try failed (11.2). Now the tight end. In week 3, Juwan Johnson scored 19.3 on my bench while Dalton Kincaid started, and the commissioner's office called me a fraud. In week 4 it happened again. This week, with Juwan back from his bye, I started Kincaid, who scored 4.3. Every time Juwan has scored more than four points this season, he's been on my bench: 60.1 points there, 4.8 in three starts. The best lineup was 160.28, and the only change is Juwan for Kincaid.
