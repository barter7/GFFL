*"What's in a name? That which we call a rose by any other name would smell as sweet." — Juliet, discussing a Romeo who scored a touchdown this week from the bench of a team named after him*

Welcome back one and all to the sixth weekly installment of the GFFL Recap! Three teams changed their names this week, and every one of them had a motive. One renamed itself after its own quarterback within two days of his 107-yard Thursday night. One changed its name after its quarterback was benched on Sunday. And one is 5-1 and may be counting. Three games were within two points going into Monday night, and the 49ers and Commanders settled all three. The Fellowship of the Ringless is 6-0, and I am 4-2 despite starting the wrong quarterback.

## Jackson Five (and One) 99.44 — Slob onmy Doubs (DoubanteAdams) 98.22

The closest game of the week (1.22; nothing else was within 13), decided by a garbage-time touchdown on Sunday and a kicker on Monday.

Thnks fr-1 th Hpprs is now **Jackson Five (and One)**. Lamar Jackson is on its IR and the record is 5-1, so take your pick. Jordan Love threw for 276 yards and ran in a touchdown in Green Bay's 23–13 win over Dallas (20.24), and Chuba Hubbard carried 28 times for 120 yards and a score in Carolina's win in Philadelphia (18.6). But the points that won it came from the *losing* side of that Packers game: down 23–7 with 52 seconds left, Dak Prescott threw a touchdown to Ryan Flournoy. It meant nothing to the Cowboys. It gave Flournoy 13.5, and without those six points Jackson Five loses by 4.78. Then Monday: Jackson Five led 95.44 to 95.02 with one player each left, Eddy Pineiro for the 49ers and Terry McLaurin for Washington. Pineiro scored 4. McLaurin scored 3.2.

Slob onmy Doubs is 0-6, and this one should sting. For the first time since week 1, the team named after Romeo Doubs benched Romeo Doubs. Doubs was targeted once, caught it for 42 yards and a touchdown from Drake Maye, and scored 10.7, while McLaurin started at receiver. Doubs for McLaurin wins by 6.28, and Jakobi Meyers (10.8) was on the bench too; the best lineup was 112.32. The name's other namesake, Davante Adams, plays for Thats My Commish and went on injured reserve Tuesday. The good news: Jaylin Noel, a bench star in weeks 4 and 5, finally got the start and caught four passes for 107 yards and a touchdown in London (18.7). Kyle Pitts, an eighth-round pick of the Charleston Kirks, caught two fourth-quarter touchdowns in a Falcons loss (20.5). Kirk Cousins threw for 313 yards (18.52), and both of his touchdowns went to Tre Tucker, who scored 24.2 for More Like Blo Dix. The Texans defense won 34–31 in London and scored -2.

## More Like Blo Dix 111.18 — The Charleston Kirks 71.8

Nix was already locked in, so a new name was the only protest available, and on Sunday Shough threw three touchdowns and ran for a fourth in New Orleans' 34–27 win over the Giants: **26.96 points on the bench**. The best lineup on this roster was 141.06, 19.26 more than anybody actually scored this week. It didn't need it. Tucker caught five passes for 97 yards and both Cousins touchdowns, the second with 1:39 left in a 33–30 loss to Buffalo (24.2). Luther Burden III had 95 yards (13.5), Hunter Henry caught a touchdown from Maye (12.8), and J.K. Dobbins scored in that Thursday game (12.1). 3-3.

The Kirks scored 71.8, the week's low, and the quarterback was fine. Maye threw three touchdown passes in New England's win over the Jets (23.8): to Doubs, to DeMario Douglas, who was on More Like Blo Dix's bench, and to Henry, who was in its lineup. Behind him: Cam Skattebo 0, Darren Waller 0, MarShawn Lloyd 1, Nick Folk 1, and Derrick Henry 4.5 with Huntley starting at quarterback for Baltimore in a 13–10 loss in Cleveland. ## Remix to Commission 114.26 — Whistlin' Willy's 92.68

I went into Monday night trailing 92.68 to 91.9. Brock Purdy threw for 264 yards in San Francisco's 23–17 win over Washington (17.36), the 49ers defense added 5, and that settled it. The Sunday work was done by the running backs. Kenneth Walker III ran 19 times for 128 yards and two touchdowns in Kansas City's 22–6 win over the Chargers (26.9, the best score in anyone's lineup). James Cook ran for 95 yards, caught three passes for 58 more and scored the go-ahead touchdown with 4:49 left in Buffalo's 33–30 win in Las Vegas (22.8). DeVonta Smith caught a touchdown from McKee, starting at quarterback for the Eagles in place of Jalen Hurts (17.0).

Why was a 21-point win close on Monday? Because last week I started Trevor Lawrence over Purdy and was right, so this week I went back to Purdy, and Lawrence threw **four touchdown passes** in London, two of them to Bhayshul Tuten, for 27.34: the highest score by any player in the league, starting or not. I'd have been done by Sunday lunch. At tight end, I finally switched from Dalton Kincaid to Juwan Johnson last week; this week Juwan scored zero and Kincaid scored 7.3. And Rhamondre Stevenson, who scored 16.9 and 16 on my bench the last two weeks, finally got the start and ran 14 times for 36 yards (3.6), while Josh Downs scored 6.7 on the bench. Three positions, three wrong guesses, each one the reverse of last week's.

Whistlin' Willy's is 3-3 and could have won this. Its best possible lineup was **115.4**, 1.14 more than mine. Alvin Kamara caught a touchdown from Shough (13.9) and Blake Corum caught one from Matthew Stafford (13.0), both on the bench while Tyler Allgeier ran 18 times for 38 yards in the flex (5.3). Caleb Williams completed 30 of 36 for 305 yards on the bench (16.5) while Patrick Mahomes threw for 272 and no touchdowns (10.48) in the game where my Walker scored twice. The bright spot was Pittsburgh: down 16–3 in the third quarter in Tampa, the Steelers won in overtime, with Chris Boswell kicking four field goals (17) and Germie Bernard catching 11 of 12 targets for 87 yards and the winning touchdown (20.2).

Chug City kept the faith, and the faith finally kicked back.
