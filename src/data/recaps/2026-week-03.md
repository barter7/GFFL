*"Insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results." — often attributed to Einstein, who never had to choose between Bo Nix and Tyler Shough*

Welcome back one and all to the third weekly installment of the GFFL Recap! Week 3 was the week the bench struck back: two games were decided by points sitting in street clothes, one team finally made the right quarterback call and still got outscored by its own bench, and Darty in the Hamptons changed its name to Smushed Hopes, which after the week they had feels less like a rebrand and more like a status update. Two teams are still unbeaten, one is still winless, and the league's first real NFL injury bills came due. Let's get to the games.

## The Fellowship of the Ringless 161.56 — (Traded my) Pick 6 98.32

The Fellowship is 3-0, leads the league in points by a lot, and this week didn't even need its quarterback. Jahmyr Gibbs, the first overall pick, went for **37.9**: two rushing touchdowns and the game-winning catch with 2:30 left in Detroit after the Jets had tied it up. Kittle added 23.2 with two late scores in San Francisco, and Garrett Wilson put up 21.7 on a 10-catch day before getting hurt on the Jets' final drive. Keep an eye on that one. Josh Allen had a strange 16.96: two rushing touchdowns wrapped around two interceptions, in a game where the Bills gave the ball away five times and still won. Jared Goff scored 19.36 on the bench, but with only one QB spot, that's a luxury, not a crime.

Pick 6 is 0-3, and the problem is at the top of the draft. **Saquon Barkley, seventh overall, 8.5 points**, in a game where the Eagles scored seven and lost by 20 to a Bears team starting a 38-year-old backup. Jalen Hurts (12.62) left briefly in the second quarter and never got going. Jakobi Meyers caught a touchdown from Trevor Lawrence and scored 15.9 on the bench, while Brian Thomas Jr. started in the flex and scored 1.3: a 14.6-point decision in a game lost by 63, so it's filed rather than prosecuted. Terry McLaurin (16.7, a Mariota touchdown) and Tee Higgins (18) were fine. The kicker was not the MVP this week, which counts as progress.

## Remix to Commission 140.88 — Chug City 85.34

Your commissioner and recapper, 3-0, presenting with total objectivity: another comfortable one. Brock Purdy — ninth round — threw four touchdowns and scored 31.28. Drake London, whom I publicly scolded last week, caught nine balls for 194 yards and scored 23.9, and I'd like to thank him for reading the column. Kenneth Walker scored twice in Miami (20.3), James Cook ran for 154 yards (19.4), and my waiver-wire kicker Tyler Loop hit a 56-yarder at the gun in Rio for 12 points. It wasn't all clean: Juwan Johnson caught two touchdowns and scored 19.3 on my bench while Dalton Kincaid started at tight end and scored 2.8. That's a 16.5-point mistake by the commissioner, and the commissioner will be hearing from himself.

Chug City's 85.34 was the lowest score of the week, and the reason is on the bench. **Jaylen Warren ran for 127 yards and scored 19.1 on the bench**, while Jadarian Price, a third-round pick, started at running back and scored 0.7. That's an 18.4-point swing, and it's the second straight week Price has disappointed on a start. Jonathan Taylor (#3 overall) managed 8.2 in a 19–17 Colts win that was mostly field goals. The kicker spot didn't help either: Trey Smack started and scored one point for a Packers team that scored 14, while Cameron Dicker made three field goals for 9 on the bench. Dak (18.94) and Olave (15.2) did their jobs. The rest is under review.

## Kim Kardashian Boutte 123.24 — Smushed Hopes 103.96

Kim K Boutte moves to 2-1 on the back of a very good draft pick. Brock Bowers made his season debut in New Orleans and scored **22.6** on 10 catches for 116 yards and a touchdown, in a comeback win from 11 down. Lamar Jackson (20.44) threw the touchdown that put the Ravens ahead late in Rio and then the 27-yard throw that set up the walk-off field goal. Ja'Marr Chase (20.3) caught a touchdown in Pittsburgh. Keaton Mitchell scored a touchdown for 12.2 on the bench while TreVeyon Henderson started for 3.4, but at a 19-point margin, it didn't matter.

Smushed Hopes — formerly Darty in the Hamptons — has the league's hottest receiver and not much else. Jaxon Smith-Njigba followed last week's 38 with **30.36**: 10 catches, 128 yards, two touchdowns, in a Seahawks loss to a Mariota-led Washington. Matthew Stafford (20.9) threw for 390 yards, but he also threw the pick-six that turned the Rams-Broncos game. Omarion Hampton, the eighth overall pick, managed 5.6 against Buffalo. Emanuel Wilson started in the flex for 1.4 while Brian Robinson Jr. (11.0, a touchdown for Atlanta) and Rachaad White (10.6) sat on the bench. That's about 10 points of bench, in a loss by 19. The new name is accurate.

## Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him 98.74 — Darn Tuten 91.24

The closest game of the week, and it was decided on Darn Tuten's bench. Michael Wilson caught 11 passes and a touchdown for Arizona and **scored 20.4 on the bench**, while Jalen Coker started in the flex and scored 2.8. That's a 17.6-point swing in a game lost by 7.5. Start Wilson and Darn Tuten is 3-0. They're 2-1 instead, third in the league in points, and the owner has to live with it. Keenan Allen (15.3) was also on the bench. Amon-Ra St. Brown managed 9.9, and the Patriots defense (the waiver add that scored 20 last week) gave up 35 in Jacksonville and scored zero.

Thats My Commish moves to 2-1, and Harold Fannin Jr. deserves the credit: a sixth-round tight end who caught two touchdowns from Deshaun Watson, including the game-winner in Cleveland with 1:55 left, for 20.6 points. Jeremiyah Love (19.4) and Davante Adams (17.2, 137 yards in the Rams' loss) helped, and Justin Herbert scored 12.74 in a game where he threw an interception at the Buffalo 1-yard line. They won despite a lot of bench points too: Kenyon Sadiq scored 20.0 on the bench and Jordan Addison caught a 41-yard touchdown for 17.5 on the bench, while Justin Jefferson (#11 overall) managed 4.2 and Tetairoa McMillan (third round) 2.7 in the lineup. When you win with 37.5 points on the bench, the column notes it and moves on.

## The Charleston Kirks 99.26 — Man Getting Hit By Football 96.58

The Kirks finally win one, by 2.68, and it cost them their first-round pick. **De'Von Achane, ninth overall, scored 1.7 and tore his ACL in the first quarter in Miami.** He's out for the season. That's the worst news any team in this league got this week, and it happened in a win. Derrick Henry scored twice in Rio (21.4), the Vikings defense had six sacks and a punt-return touchdown for 19 points, and the Kirks held on. Drake Maye (3.76) was benched in the fourth quarter of a 35–6 loss, and Wan'Dale Robinson scored 15.2 on the bench while Xavier Worthy started for 3.0. That one didn't matter this week. Next week it might.

Man Getting Hit By Football lost by 2.68, and **Cooper Kupp scored 12.6 on the bench, including a late touchdown in Washington**, while Malik Nabers (5.1) and Stefon Diggs (5.3) started. Kupp over Nabers is 7.5 points, which would have won the game. Joe Burrow scored 22.58 before his strip-sack in the final minute in Pittsburgh. CMC added 19.6 and Deebo Samuel 15.4, including an 80-yard catch-and-run on a lateral from Mike Evans. Then there's the defense: the Eagles D/ST scored **negative one**, allowing 27 points to a team starting Case Keenum. In a game lost by 2.68, that stings.

## Immaculate Concepcion 123.20 — Whistlin' Willy's 100.64

A follow-up on the league's longest-running story. For two weeks, Immaculate Concepcion started Bo Nix over Tyler Shough and lost both times while Shough outscored Nix on the bench. This week they started Shough. He threw four touchdowns in a Saints loss and scored 23.8. Bo Nix, on the bench, led Denver back from 16–0 down, ran in the game-winner with 48 seconds left, and scored **24.14**. That's 0.34 points. After two weeks of wrong answers, the right answer came one week too late. The good news is it didn't cost them anything: Bijan Robinson ran for 194 yards and two touchdowns (34.3) in Atlanta's win at Lambeau, Luther Burden caught a touchdown from Keenum (16.3), Harrison Mevis kicked four field goals for 16, and Immaculate Concepcion won its first game of the season. The file on Nix-vs-Shough stays open.

Whistlin' Willy's did learn from last week: Patrick Mahomes, the waiver pickup who sat on the bench for 29 points, started this week and scored 15.94 in a win in Miami. The Williams backfield (Kyren 18.8, Javonte 17.3) did well, and Chris Boswell kicked the tying and go-ahead field goals in Pittsburgh for 13. The receivers didn't show up: Jaylen Waddle 5.4, Chris Godwin 4.0, Tucker Kraft 4.6, Ladd McConkey 8.6. Caleb Williams scored zero on the bench, where he watched a 38-year-old backup take his job for a week and beat the Eagles by 20.
