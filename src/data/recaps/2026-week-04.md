*"You miss 100% of the shots you don't take." — Wayne Gretzky, who never had to decide between Josh Downs and Ollie Gordon*

Welcome back one and all to the fourth weekly installment of the GFFL Recap! If you added up every point that sat on a bench this week, you'd have a team that beats everybody in the league, and it isn't close. Three teams left more than 37 points on their benches. Two games would have flipped with a better lineup, one of them by twelve hundredths of a point. The Fellowship of the Ringless is the last unbeaten team, the commissioner took his first loss and deserved it, and one manager started a quarterback who left in the first quarter while his other quarterback beat the Bills. Let's get to it.

## The Fellowship of the Ringless 119.32 — Remix to Commission 101.22

A first-place showdown between two 3-0 teams, and I'd like to start by saying the better team won. Not the better roster. The better *lineup*. Those are different things, as I am about to explain at my own expense.

The Fellowship is 4-0 and won comfortably without its two best performers, both of whom were on the bench. Kyle Monangai ran 30 times for **146 yards and two touchdowns** (27.5) in the Bears' win over the Jets, taking D'Andre Swift's job in the process, while Swift started at running back and scored 6.4. George Kittle took a short pass from Brock Purdy 56 yards for a touchdown (15.0) while Travis Kelce started at tight end and scored 2.5. Jared Goff threw for 412 yards and scored 20.48 on the bench behind Josh Allen's 18.52. All told, the Fellowship left **43.16 points** on the bench, the most in the league, and still won by 18. That's what 588 points in four weeks looks like. Zay Flowers (21.8, a touchdown in Baltimore), Ka'imi Fairbairn (16) and the Seahawks defense (12, four takeaways from the Chargers) carried the lineup, and Jahmyr Gibbs (15.7) took a week off by his standards.

And now, the commissioner. Remix to Commission's best possible lineup this week was **141.72**, which beats the Fellowship by 22. The actual lineup scored 101.22. Ollie Gordon ran for 100 yards and a touchdown in Minnesota (17.0) and Rhamondre Stevenson scored in the Patriots' upset of Buffalo (16.9), both on the bench, while Josh Downs (3.1) and Jacory Croskey-Merritt (6.0) started. Then there's tight end, where for the second straight week Juwan Johnson outscored Dalton Kincaid from the bench, 8.9 to 1.2. I said last week the commissioner would be hearing from himself. He did, and he ignored himself. Kenneth Walker III, my keeper, was the bright spot: **177 rushing yards** and two late touchdowns in Kansas City's win in Las Vegas, the second of them 22 yards after a fumbled kickoff, for 30.4. Purdy (19.62) threw his two touchdowns to Deebo Samuel and George Kittle, both of whom spent the day on other people's benches, which is the closest this lineup came to a theme. James Cook (15.3) scored in Buffalo's loss. 3-1, and the loss is entirely self-inflicted.

## Smushed Hopes 107.98 — The Charleston Kirks 64.54

The biggest margin of the week (43.44), and it was mostly decided in the first quarter in London. The Kirks started Marcus Mariota. Mariota dropped back five times, ran once for 35 yards and left in the first quarter with a sprained MCL. He scored 5.34. On the bench, Drake Maye threw three touchdowns and ran for 56 yards in Buffalo, including a 23-yard touchdown to Efton Chism with 1:55 left to beat the Bills, and scored **26.16**. That's a 20.82-point quarterback decision, and it wasn't even the only problem: Rashee Rice didn't record a play and scored zero in the flex, and the Kirks' 64.54 is the second-lowest score of the season, two hundredths of a point above the floor. Derrick Henry (14.9, a touchdown in Baltimore) was the top scorer. The best lineup the Kirks could have fielded was 100.96, which still loses, so this one goes in the file as a bad week, not a bench crime. Mostly.

Smushed Hopes moves to 2-2 with a bench story of its own. Last week Emanuel Wilson started in the flex and scored 1.4. This week he went to the bench and scored **25.5**, with two touchdowns in Seattle's win over the Chargers. Brian Robinson Jr. sat next to him and scored three touchdowns in Atlanta's 45–24 win in New Orleans, for 25.2. That's 50.7 points on the bench while Najee Harris started in the flex and scored 3.0. It didn't matter. T.J. Hockenson caught **13 passes on 13 targets** for 119 yards (18.4), Carnell Tate had 145 receiving yards for the Titans (17.0), and Ashton Jeanty added 15.6. Jaxon Smith-Njigba came back to earth at 10.1 after 38 and 30. Smushed Hopes has now been punished for its lineup and rewarded for it in consecutive weeks, which is the most Smushed Hopes thing possible.

## Chug City 126.20 — Man Getting Hit By Football 113.02

The closest game of the week, and it came down to one decision. Man Getting Hit By Football started DJ Moore at receiver. Moore caught one pass in Buffalo and scored 2.2. Deebo Samuel, on the bench, caught a touchdown from Purdy and ran for 44 yards (15.5). Swap those two and Man Getting Hit scores 126.32, which beats 126.20 by **0.12 points**. Twelve hundredths. Joe Burrow threw for 428 yards (23.72), Nico Collins scored twice (27.3), Malik Nabers caught a touchdown (20.2), and the team is 1-3 because of a choice between two wide receivers. Kendre Miller (0.9) started at running back in a game where his teammate Alvin Kamara scored twice, which didn't help either. CJ Stroud scored 23.08 on the bench, but Burrow outscored him by 0.64, so the quarterback call was the right one. It was the only one.

Chug City is 2-2 with a balanced lineup and one recurring kicker problem. Jonathan Taylor ran for two touchdowns in London (22.2), Dak Prescott (18.1) led the 74-yard drive that beat Houston with 43 seconds left, and Chris Olave caught eight passes for 116 yards (15.6) in a Saints loss. Jaylen Warren, last week's bench crime, started this week and ran for 93 yards (14.1). Waiver pickup Devaughn Vele caught a touchdown (14.4). Rome Odunze had 94 yards (12.4). And for the second straight week, Trey Smack started at kicker over Cameron Dicker, and Dicker outscored him from the bench, 12 to 7. At some point it's a policy.

## Darn Tuten 133.26 — I AM JACK KERLEY 101.82

Last week Darn Tuten left Michael Wilson on the bench for 20.4 points and lost by 7.5. This week Wilson started and scored 13.0, and Darn Tuten won by 31. Lesson learned, one week late. CeeDee Lamb did most of the work: **17 catches for 189 yards** and the game-winning 12-yard touchdown from Dak with 43 seconds left in Houston, for 32.8. (Chug City got the Dak half of that same play. One throw, two fantasy wins.) Waiver quarterback Bryce Young threw for 329 yards and two touchdowns in Carolina's upset of Detroit (21.46), Cam Little made three field goals in Cincinnati (14) and Amon-Ra St. Brown added 11.5. Darn Tuten is 3-1 and second in the league in points.

The team formerly known as (Traded my) Pick 6 has a new name, I AM JACK KERLEY, which this column will not attempt to explain, and the same record: 0-4. The new name did not come with new lineup habits. **Terry McLaurin started in the flex and scored zero.** He was ruled inactive hours before kickoff in London, a day after reports said he'd likely miss it. Jaylin Noel scored 10.4 on the bench. Saquon Barkley, the seventh overall pick, hurt his hamstring after two carries in Philadelphia and scored 1.5 (the early word is that it's not serious). Jalen Hurts threw for 93 yards (13.52) in a game the Eagles led 20–10 with seven and a half minutes left and lost. Tee Higgins (21.2, 157 yards), Romeo Doubs (20.8, two touchdowns in the Patriots' upset) and Sam LaPorta (18.4) did their part. It wasn't enough, and with a 31-point loss, the McLaurin start is only the most avoidable part of a bad day.

## Whistlin' Willy's 129.90 — Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him 107.06

Whistlin' Willy's has two running backs named Williams, and this week they scored 60.5 points between them. Kyren Williams had 147 yards from scrimmage and two touchdowns (31.7), the second one the go-ahead score with 1:17 left in Philadelphia after the Rams trailed 20–10 with a 5% chance to win. Javonte Williams ran for **three touchdowns** in Dallas's win in Houston (28.8). Alvin Kamara added two more touchdowns in a Saints blowout loss (20.3), and Patrick Mahomes threw two (17.0) in a 30–27 win in Las Vegas. Willy's is 2-2. The only blemishes: Ladd McConkey, playing through a foot injury, was targeted twice and caught nothing (0.0), and Blake Corum started in the flex for 2.6 while Brenton Strange caught seven balls for 95 yards on the bench (13.0). Neither mattered in a 23-point win.

Thats My Commish had the best player in the league this week and lost by 23. **Tetairoa McMillan caught 14 of 16 targets for 192 yards and two touchdowns** for 38.2 points, the highest score of the week, in Carolina's upset of the Lions. Nobody else broke 10.2. Justin Herbert (9.56) threw two interceptions and fumbled on a sack in Seattle as the Chargers fell to 0-4. Jeremiyah Love managed 6.0 against a projection of 14.2, and Davante Adams caught four passes for 32 yards (5.2). Justin Jefferson is on IR with an ankle injury. Thats My Commish is 2-2, with one wide receiver doing the work of five.

## Kim Kardashian Boutte 138.58 — Immaculate Concepcion 112.24

The highest score of the week belongs to Kim Kardashian Boutte, and the best part was a seventh-round pick. Chuba Hubbard ran for 122 yards and two touchdowns in Carolina's 32–26 win over Detroit (25.4). Lamar Jackson completed 15 of 20 for 222 yards and two touchdowns in one half before leaving with an ankle injury (18.88; he's listed as day-to-day). Quinshon Judkins (18.6) scored in Cleveland's walk-off win over Pittsburgh on Thursday, kicker Jake Bates kicked four field goals for 18 in a Lions *loss*, and Brock Bowers added 17.6. The one quiet spot was the fourth overall pick: Ja'Marr Chase caught three passes for 27 yards (4.2) on a day Burrow threw 54 times and Tee Higgins got 16 targets. RJ Harvey caught all 10 of his targets for 14.3 on the bench while TreVeyon Henderson started and scored 4.2, but in a 26-point win, nobody's checking. Kim K Boutte is 3-1.

And now, an update on the league's longest-running story. For two weeks, Immaculate Concepcion started Bo Nix and Tyler Shough was the better quarterback. Last week they switched to Shough, and Nix outscored him by 0.34. This week they started Shough again, and **Shough outscored Nix, 15.94 to 13.56.** After four weeks the front office finally made the right quarterback call in the right week, and they lost by 26, because Bijan Robinson (27.2, 145 yards and two touchdowns) ran all over Shough's Saints for the other team in the same game. Puka Nacua caught nine passes for 125 yards and ran in a touchdown (23.2). Trey McBride caught seven passes for 31 yards on 12 targets (6.6), and David Montgomery managed 4.3. Immaculate Concepcion is 1-3, and the Nix-vs-Shough file is closed.
