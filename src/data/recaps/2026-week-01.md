*"Power is not a means; it is an end." — George Orwell, describing a man who is both recapper and commissioner of two leagues*

Welcome back one and all to the first weekly installment of the GFFL Recap! The recap empire expands. First the auction league got a column, and now the GFFL — the flagship, the original, the league where friendships go to be tested — gets its recaps back. For the newcomers to this format: I go game by game, I celebrate the studs, I investigate the crimes, and I apply the sacred editorial policy adapted for fantasy purposes: roasts are indexed to **draft capital and projections**, and because somebody will ask: the cutoff is **rounds 1 through 5**. Spend a pick in the first five rounds and you have bought yourself full roast liability. Round 6 and later, you are judged on projection alone — if your 14th-round flier scored 4 points, that's not a bust, that's a lottery ticket doing lottery ticket things. And note that the capital in question is *ours*, not the NFL's. This column does not care where Marvin Harrison Jr. went in the real draft; it cares that he went 65th in ours. And with that said, let's get to the games.

## The Fellowship of the Ringless 174.66 — Immaculate Concepcion 117.24

We start with the biggest number of the week, because 174.66 demands to be started with. This roster looked less like a fantasy team and more like a war crime: Josh Allen 35.66 (a fifth-round QB, which should be illegal), Jahmyr Gibbs 31.1 (the #1 overall pick doing #1 overall things — no points for creativity, full points for points), and D'Andre Swift adding 31.9 from the third round. Three players over 30. Most teams this week didn't have *one*. And here's the beautiful part — even in a 57-point massacre, the crime log isn't empty: Kyle Monangai's 19.4 rode the bench while Tony Pollard delivered 3.9 in the flex. Didn't matter. Nothing mattered. But I see everything, and it goes in the log. As for Immaculate Concepcion — 117 is a respectable score that happened to walk into a wood chipper. Bijan (#2 overall) put up 27.3 against a pregame projection of 16.8, David Montgomery chipped in 27.4, and none of it was the problem. The problem was starting Bo Nix for 5.44 while Tyler Shough sat on the bench with 23.2 — an 18-point QB decision that turns a blowout into a... slightly smaller blowout. The editorial policy notes the loss was unavoidable; the QB room notes it's on notice. Rico Dowdle (3.1 against a projection of 11.6) rounds out the misery.

## Remix to Commission 133.7 — The Charleston Kirks 92.02

Your commissioner and recapper, winning his debut, and I promise the following analysis is completely objective: what a masterclass. Kenneth Walker III — a fourth-round *keeper*, the kind of value that makes the league office (me) proud of the league office (also me) — dropped 32.6, and Trevor Lawrence, an eighth-round pick projected for 16.9, delivered 26.1. That's the whole formula: keeper value plus late-round QB juice. The one blemish: Drake London, second-round pick, 4.5 points against an 11-point projection. That's a capital-indexed roast and I do not exempt myself — London, you embarrassed me in front of my own column. Across the field, the Charleston Kirks are this week's tragedy: Derrick Henry went for 34.8 — third-highest score in the entire league, second-round pick fully justifying himself — and the rest of the starting lineup combined for 57. A wasted masterpiece. Somewhere, King Henry is running through a tackler who isn't even there, out of pure habit, for a team that scored 92. Cam Skattebo's 14.1 from round 11 deserves mention as the only supporting actor who read the script.

## Man Getting Hit By Football 137.56 — (Traded my) Pick 6 91.22

The Simpsons taught us that the Man Getting Hit By Football works on so many levels, and this week the level was "comfortably." Dallas Goedert (round 8) put up 21.7, the roster hummed along to 137, and the only ding in the log is benching Deebo's 15 for Nabers' 9.9 — a five-point misdemeanor, barely worth the paperwork. Now, Pick 6. *Deep breath.* I want you to sit down for this next sentence, although based on the box score you're already lying down: your most valuable player this week was Evan McPherson. Your kicker. Pick number one hundred and ninety-nine. He scored 19 of your 91.22 points — that's over 20% of the offense, from the leg. Chase Brown (round 8) added a respectable 16.3, and then the log shows Terry McLaurin starting with 2.4 while Jakobi Meyers sat with 11.2. When the lowest score in the league, a kicker-MVP situation, AND a bench crime appear in the same box score, we don't call it a loss. We call it a *collection*.

## Darn Tuten 157.96 — Darty in the Hamptons 119.8

And now, the week's greatest paradox: the second-highest score in the league, a 38-point blowout win — and simultaneously the single largest Bench Crime in this young season's history. Darn Tuten benched Jalen Coker. Jalen Coker, round 10, scored **29.8 points** — which, had he played, would have been a top-seven score in the entire league across all rosters. In his place, Michael Wilson started and produced 8.1. Also on the bench: Dontayvion Wicks, 14.3. That's 36 points left in street clothes *in a game you won by 38*, which means Darn Tuten came within a rounding error of committing the perfect crime — leaving more points on the bench than the margin of victory. What actually played was plenty: Christian Watson went nuclear for 29.7 from round 6 and Isaiah Likely (round 9) added 23.8. This team's floor is high and its bench might be higher, which is terrifying. Darty in the Hamptons, meanwhile, got a beautiful 29.7 from second-rounder Ashton Jeanty and then quietly lost the game in the flex, where Marvin Harrison Jr. delivered 3.8 while Denzel Boston (12.9) and T.J. Hockenson (11.6) watched from the bench. And before anyone reaches for the pitchforks: Harrison went in the *sixth round* here, four picks after Caleb Williams, so the policy grants him full immunity from capital roasting. This isn't a bad pick that failed. It's a fine pick that got started over two better options, which is a different sin and arguably the more annoying one. The Hamptons is a nice place to summer; it is apparently a rough place to set a lineup.

## Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him 109.86 — Chug City 100.8

The closest game of the week, decided by 9.06, and I regret to inform everyone that it was decided by a *defense*. The Steelers D/ST — round 13, projected for 7.2 — put up 19, which was the entire margin with change left over. Justin Jefferson did first-rounder things with 27.2, and that plus the Pittsburgh ambush survived a genuinely alarming ledger: Davante Adams gave a fourth-round 4.1 against an 11.8 projection (capital roast: applied), Brock Purdy scored 21.1 on the bench while Justin Herbert started for 13.26, and Pat Freiermuth's 13.1 sat behind a 3.1-point starter. That's roughly 18 bench points in a nine-point game — a heart attack speedrun that happened to end in a W, and in this league a W absolves almost everything. Almost. Chug City will be sick about this one: the game was *right there*, and the log shows DK Metcalf starting at 6 while Aaron Jones scored 10 on the bench. Not enough to flip it alone, but in a one-score fantasy game every decision gets the microscope, and this microscope is unkind. 100.8 with no standout performance is the quietest way to lose — nobody to blame is somehow worse than somebody to blame.

## Whistlin' Willy's 124.96 — Kim Kardashian Boutte 100.66

We close with the week's headline steal: Caleb Williams, sixth round, pick 61, **37.26 points** — the highest score by any player in the entire league. Every quarterback drafted before him is now legally required to be compared to him weekly, and I will personally administer the comparisons. Add Javonte Williams' 21.7 from round 10 and Whistlin' Willy's built a comfortable win almost entirely out of value picks, which is the good-process-good-outcome double that fantasy dreams are made of. The one wobble: Chris Godwin's 6.3 started over Jordan Mason's 11.9 — a minor infraction in a week this clean. For Kim Kardashian Boutte, the box score giveth little: the 17th-round Jaguars D/ST outkicked its projection with 14 (genuinely nice), but Quinshon Judkins — a round-14 keeper projected at 12.2 — managed 6.0, and 100.66 was never threatening 124. The team name remains elite. The teams in the name did not play like it.

## Superlatives

**Highest Scoring Team:** The Fellowship of the Ringless (174.66) — three 30-point players, one very unnecessary bench crime.

**Lowest Scoring Team:** (Traded my) Pick 6 (91.22) — the kicker-as-MVP week we'll be referencing all season.

**Highest Scoring Player:** Caleb Williams (Whistlin' Willy's) — 37.26, round 6, pick 61. The steal audit is closed; he won.

**Bench Crime of the Week:** Jalen Coker, 29.8 points, benched (Darn Tuten) — would have been top-seven league-wide. The perfect crime, foiled only by winning anyway.

**Wasted Masterpiece:** Derrick Henry, 34.8 (The Charleston Kirks) — a second-round pick playing championship football for a team that scored 92.

**Draft Capital Bonfire:** Terry McLaurin 2.4 (round 4), Tony Pollard 3.9 (round 4), Davante Adams 4.1 (round 4), Drake London 4.5 (round 2). Four starters taken inside the cutoff, four scores you could fit on one hand. Round 4 was apparently cursed leaguewide and I'd like it investigated.

**QB Decision of the Week (Dishonorable):** Bo Nix 5.44 started over Tyler Shough 23.2 (Immaculate Concepcion). Eighteen points is not a decision, it's a donation.

## Standings

Week 1 winners' circle: **The Fellowship of the Ringless, Darn Tuten, Man Getting Hit By Football, Remix to Commission, Whistlin' Willy's, Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him** — all 1-0. The other six are 0-1, and per league tradition, all preseason confidence is hereby void.
