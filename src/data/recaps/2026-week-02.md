*"The only thing we have to fear is fear itself — and the waiver wire quarterback you left on your bench." — Franklin D. Roosevelt, lightly edited*

Welcome back one and all to the second weekly installment of the GFFL Recap! Week 1 was about the studs. Week 2, it turns out, was about the *lineups* — a league-wide outbreak of starting the wrong guy so severe that three different teams started a quarterback who was outscored by the one on their own bench, and one of them did it for the second week in a row with the same two quarterbacks. We'll get there. For the newcomers who skipped the syllabus: I go game by game, I celebrate the studs, I investigate the crimes, and the editorial policy is indexed to **draft capital and projections** with a cutoff at **rounds 1 through 5**. Spend a top-five-round pick and you have bought full roast liability; round 6 and later, you are judged on projection alone. And one clarification on the bench ledger, because the lineup has a fixed number of spots: a bench score only counts against you if that player could have taken a spot from a *lower-scoring starter he was eligible to replace*. Two quarterbacks scoring 40 and 27 is not a crime, it's a luxury. One quarterback scoring 8 while the other scores 29 on the bench — that's a crime. And with Monday night in the books — the Rams beat the Giants 28–6, a result that mattered enormously to exactly one lineup in this league — let's get to the games.

## The Fellowship of the Ringless 132.52 — Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him 110.78

Two weeks, two wins, and the same engine both times: Josh Allen, the fifth-round quarterback, put up **40.82** — the highest score by any player in the league this week, against a pregame projection of 22.7. That's 18 points of pure surplus from a pick that cost the Fellowship a fifth-rounder, and I'd like every QB drafted in rounds one through four to please form an orderly line for their weekly comparison. Gibbs (20.3) and Kittle (16) did the rest, and the Fellowship sits 2-0 with the second-most points in the league. Now, the log. Zay Flowers — a seventh-round *keeper* — started and scored **zero**, while Rashod Bateman, round 17, scored 18.3 on the bench. Same team, same game, same offense; one of them was inactive and the other was catching passes. Matthew Stafford's 26.98 also sat on the bench, and per the clarification above that's no crime at all — one QB spot, and the one in it scored 40. That's the luxury case. Bateman over Flowers is the crime case. Across the field, a genuine redemption arc: Davante Adams, roasted in this very column seven days ago for a fourth-round 4.1, went out on Monday night and dropped **35.5**, the third-highest score in the league. Consider the roast rescinded, with interest. The problem was everything else — Justin Jefferson (#11 overall) managed 7, Jeremiyah Love (#14 overall) 6 against an 11.3 projection, Kenny Gainwell started for 3 while Jonah Coleman scored 13.3 on the bench — and Harrison Butker was the second-leading scorer at 16. When your kicker is your #2 and your Monday night hero still loses by 22, the roster has a distribution problem.

## Remix to Commission 148.68 — Man Getting Hit By Football 73.78

Your commissioner and recapper, now 2-0, and I will once again be presenting this analysis with total objectivity: a *rout*. Brock Purdy — ninth round, pick 107 — threw for 28.48; DeVonta Smith (22.7), Kenneth Walker (20.8, the keeper that keeps on keeping), James Cook (20.4) and an 11th-round Dalton Kincaid (19 against a projection of 8.4) rounded out a lineup with five players at 19 or better. The log is not empty, because the log is never empty: Drake London, second-round pick, 6.9 points. That's 11.4 through two weeks from the 19th overall selection, and the editorial policy requires me to note that I am the one who selected him. London, we need to talk. Rhamondre Stevenson (round 5) chipped in 3.1 in the flex while Josh Downs sat with 10.7 — a misdemeanor in a 75-point win, but filed. And now Man Getting Hit By Football, last week's comfortable winner, this week's cautionary tale. 73.78 is bad. The *shape* of 73.78 is worse. Malik Nabers (round 3) scored 0.6. Dallas Goedert scored 0.9. DJ Moore (round 4, projected 10.5) scored **negative 0.1**, which I did not know was possible in this format and have now confirmed is possible in this format. Three starters, 1.4 points combined. Meanwhile, on the bench: Stefon Diggs 19.2 and Jake Ferguson 18.3 — 37 points sitting behind 1.5 at the same two positions, a receiver for a receiver and a tight end for a tight end. It wouldn't have won the game — nothing was winning this game — but it would have made the score look like a football result instead of a *baseball* result. CMC's 20.6 was the lone adult in the room.

## Chug City 126.66 — (Traded my) Pick 6 93.96

Chug City, 0-1 and quiet last week, found the volume knob: Dak Prescott (round 7) went for 29.76, Jonathan Taylor (#3 overall) for 27.2, and Chris Olave (round 2) for 18.6. That's your one-two-three and it's a good one. The only ding is a familiar one — Dalton Schultz scored 20 on the bench while Tyler Warren started at TE for 11.4 — and a new one: Jadarian Price, the third-round rookie, was projected for 12.3 and produced 4.5. That's inside the cutoff, so the roast applies, though I'll note in his defense that Seattle appears to be running a committee and the projection appears to have been written by his agent. Pick 6, meanwhile, drops to 0-2 in the most Pick 6 way imaginable. Last week the kicker was the MVP. This week Evan McPherson's 12 was merely *tied* for third on the team, which is progress the way a slightly smaller fire is progress. The headline crime is a capital one: **Saquon Barkley, seventh overall pick, 2.5 points.** Two and a half. Philadelphia won the game; Saquon apparently attended it. Terry McLaurin (round 4) followed last week's 2.4 with a 6, and Jakobi Meyers started in the flex for 3.3 while Tank Bigsby scored 10.8 on the bench. Sam LaPorta (14.2 on a 9 projection) and Tee Higgins (12) did their jobs. The rest of the roster is under investigation.

## Darty in the Hamptons 115.1 — Whistlin' Willy's 79.62

The Hamptons bounced back, and they did it on the shoulders of one man: Jaxon Smith-Njigba, a third-round *keeper*, **38 points** against a projection of 15.2 — the second-highest score in the league and the single largest beat of a projection all week. Add Travis Kelce (20.6 from round 8), Omarion Hampton (16.5) and a 16-point Brandon Aubrey and the win was comfortable, which is fortunate, because the rest of the lineup was a horror movie. Jaxson Dart started at quarterback on Monday night, watched the Giants score six points in Los Angeles, and returned **0.8**. Not 8. Zero point eight. Devin Singletary added 0.9 in one flex spot and Carnell Tate 4.2 in the other, while Denzel Boston (18) and Rachaad White (11.3) rode the bench — 29 points in street clothes behind two flex starters who combined for 5.1, and both swaps were legal. Tate, a fifth-rounder, also goes on the capital ledger for the 4.2. The Hamptons remain a lovely place to summer and a chaotic place to set a lineup, but 1-1 with JSN on the roster is a fine place to be. As for Whistlin' Willy's: *deep breath.* Last week Caleb Williams was this column's darling, 37.26 points, a sixth-round steal, the whole bit. This week he scored 7.72. That happens; quarterbacks have bad weeks. What does *not* just happen is picking up Patrick Mahomes off the waiver wire, watching him score **28.98**, and leaving him on the bench behind the 7.72. That's a 21-point quarterback decision, and per policy a free-agent pickup carries no draft capital, which means the only capital being burned here is *decision-making* capital. Jaylen Waddle (17.8) and Kyren Williams (14.7, on Monday night) played well; Travis Etienne (round 2, 6.1), Ladd McConkey (round 4, 5.0) and Tucker Kraft (round 5, 2.5) did not, and Blake Corum's 9.7 sat behind both flex starters. Mahomes alone doesn't flip this game. But 79.62 with a 29-point quarterback on the bench is the kind of box score that gets screenshotted.

## Kim Kardashian Boutte 86.5 — The Charleston Kirks 73.22

The lowest-scoring game of the week, and both teams are going to want to skip this paragraph. Kim K Boutte gets the win, and Ja'Marr Chase (#4 overall, 23 points) is the reason — the first-rounder doing first-rounder things while Lamar Jackson (14.8) and Chuba Hubbard (13.4) provided competent support. And then the log, which for a winning team is unusually long: Jameson Williams (round 4) scored 4.3 against a 10.4 projection, Quentin Johnston started in the flex for 1.9 while TreVeyon Henderson scored 13.6 on the bench, and Michael Mayer started at tight end for 3.8 while Terrance Ferguson scored 14.4 on the bench. That's 28 bench points left behind two starters who scored 5.7 — in a game won by 13, which makes it the rare crime that would have merely *padded* the margin. The Kirks are 0-2 with the fewest points in the league, and the box score explains why: Derrick Henry — 34.8 last week — came back to earth at 16.2, De'Von Achane (#9 overall) managed 10.8, and the rest of the lineup was a rounding error. Colston Loveland (round 4) scored 0.8 at tight end, Courtland Sutton 4 at receiver, and Xavier Worthy's 11 sat on the bench behind Sutton. Drake Maye watched his Patriots win 20–3 and contributed 8.02 to the cause. Two weeks, 165 points, and the only 30-point performance on the roster came in a loss. The Kirks need a week where the lineup and the results happen at the same time.

## Darn Tuten 156.44 — Immaculate Concepcion 64.52

The highest score of the week met the lowest score of the week, and the result was a 91.92-point margin, which is not a fantasy football score so much as a *zip code*. Darn Tuten — 2-0, most points in the league, and an entirely different team from last week's bench-crime headliner — started everyone who mattered: CeeDee Lamb (#10 overall) 31.3, Amon-Ra St. Brown (second-round keeper) 30.7 against a 16.5 projection, and the *Patriots defense*, a free-agent add, 20 points. The namesake himself, Bhayshul Tuten, chipped in 13.2 from round 3. And — I want this on the record — Jalen Coker, last week's Bench Crime of the Week, was promoted to the starting lineup and scored 10.6. Lessons were learned. Bryce Young's 24.08 sat on the bench behind Jayden Daniels' 14.74, but when you win by 92 the log is a formality. Immaculate Concepcion, however. *Sit down.* You may recall that last week this column flagged the decision to start Bo Nix (5.44) over Tyler Shough (23.2) as an eighteen-point donation. This week: Bo Nix started again, scored 14.12, and Tyler Shough scored 22.38 on the bench *again*. That's back-to-back weeks, same two quarterbacks, same wrong answer — a 26-point cumulative decision and the first repeat offense in recap history. It wasn't alone: Tre Tucker (round 13) scored 20.4 on the bench while Alec Pierce (round 7) started for 1.6 and Luther Burden (round 5) for 4.7. Bijan Robinson, the second overall pick, managed 9.6; David Montgomery (round 4) 3.4. Immaculate Concepcion has now started the wrong quarterback twice, lost twice, and scored the fewest points in a single week this season. The editorial policy notes the opponent scored 156 and the loss was unavoidable. The editorial policy also notes that 64 is 64.

## Superlatives

**Highest Scoring Team:** Darn Tuten (156.44) — two 30-point receivers, a 20-point defense off the wire, and last week's bench crime promoted to starter. Growth.

**Lowest Scoring Team:** Immaculate Concepcion (64.52) — the lowest score of the season, with 20 and 22 points on the bench.

**Highest Scoring Player:** Josh Allen (The Fellowship of the Ringless) — 40.82, round 5, pick 49. The first quarterback off the board is also the best one, which is either superb scouting or a league that waited until round five to draft a quarterback. Both, probably.

**Bench Crime of the Week:** Patrick Mahomes, 28.98 points, benched (Whistlin' Willy's) — a waiver pickup who outscored the starting quarterback by 21.3. Honorable mention to Stefon Diggs (19.2) and Jake Ferguson (18.3) sitting behind 0.6 and 0.9 for Man Getting Hit By Football.

**Repeat Offender of the Week:** Bo Nix over Tyler Shough, Immaculate Concepcion — two weeks, two wrong answers, 26 points. The commissioner's office has opened a file.

**Wasted Masterpiece:** Davante Adams, 35.5 (Thats My Commish Do Ya Know Him) — a Monday-night redemption performance for a team that had already lost by Sunday evening. Last week's roast is formally withdrawn.

**Draft Capital Bonfire:** Saquon Barkley 2.5 (#7 overall), DJ Moore −0.1 (round 4), Malik Nabers 0.6 (round 3), Colston Loveland 0.8 (round 4), Rhamondre Stevenson 3.1 (round 5), David Montgomery 3.4 (round 4). Six starters inside the cutoff, ten combined points. Barkley alone accounts for a quarter of them.

**Beat of the Week:** Jaxon Smith-Njigba, 38 against a 15.2 projection (Darty in the Hamptons) — 22.8 points of surplus from a third-round keeper.

**Monday Night Ledger:** Davante Adams 35.5, Matthew Stafford 26.98 (on a bench), Kyren Williams 14.7, Isaiah Likely 5.8, Jaxson Dart 0.8 (in a starting lineup). The Giants scored six points and this league felt every one of them.
